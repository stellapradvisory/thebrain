“Okay, but where does Zero Click Marketing actually happen?”
Zero Click Marketing tends to thrive in
four specific environments
on the modern internet — places where people can learn, form opinions, and build trust without needing to click a link.
Once you look at these environments through the ZCM lens, you’ll never unsee it.

The 4 environments of Zero-Click Marketing
1. Social feeds
The most obvious place Zero Click Marketing happens is social feeds: LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram, X.
Feeds are built for native consumption. Platforms want users to stay in the feed, so they reward content that delivers value right there.
That’s why:
long LinkedIn posts often outperform link posts
TikTok creators explain ideas directly in the video
threads summarize ideas instead of just linking out to blogs
Instead of sending people out of the app, you’re delivering the value inside the platform. And when someone finds that valuable, they remember you. They follow you. They trust you.
Then later, when they need a product, service, or recommendation… they search for you directly.
That’s Zero Click Marketing.
2. Video platforms
Video platforms like YouTube and TikTok are another major ZCM environment. (I’m giving TikTok double duty here as both a social network and video platform because, well, it’s both. If you’re on the app, I would wager the majority of accounts you follow aren’t actually your friends.)
Someone can watch a 10-minute explanation and learn everything they need without clicking anything at all.
One of my favorite recent examples comes from my friend Scott Heimendinger, founder of
Seattle Ultrasonics
. Scott recently launched a new kitchen tool built around ultrasonic blade technology. Commercial ultrasonic blades already exist in industrial settings — for example, slicing bread evenly before packaging — but Scott adapted the technology into something usable for home cooks: a chef’s knife with an ultrasonic blade.
During launch week, he published a roughly
10-minute YouTube video
explaining the entire concept: the tech, the problem it solves, and why it matters.
That video alone was enough to persuade thousands of people (literally, nearly a quarter-million views in its first week) that the product was worth paying attention to.
The explanation itself became the marketing.
Aside from the massive views,
Seattle Ultrasonics
sold out of preorders within about two months.
That’s Zero Click Marketing in action.
The persuasion happened inside the content, long before someone ever visited the website.
3. Communities
Another place ZCM thrives is communities. Places like:
Reddit
Slack groups
Discord communities
WhatsApp group chats
In these environments, the valuable thing isn’t traffic. It’s conversation.
People reference tools, brands, and creators constantly. Sometimes they link. Often they don’t.
Someone might say:
“SparkToro has good research on this.”
Or:
“I learned that from Amanda’s post.”
Those mentions shape decisions even though no measurable click happened. (No, really, even when you get clicks from that 1,000-person Slack community, it’ll show up as Direct in your GA4.
Thanks, Dark Social
.)
This is one of the reasons attribution is getting harder across the internet. Influence spreads through conversations that analytics tools simply cannot see.
4. AI answers
And now there’s a fourth environment emerging quickly: AI answers.
When someone asks ChatGPT or Claude for recommendations, they often receive a summarized answer without visiting any of the sources. The LLM synthesizes information, mentions brands, and references ideas. That informs what people believe and what they eventually choose.
In
a recent episode of the podcast
, I talked about this with Rand Fishkin.
One of the things we discussed is AI visibility — how often a brand appears in those answers. (And btw, we think it may be reasonable to track visibility! You can learn more in
his recent study on AI ranking
and visibility.)
Again: influence without the click.
The pattern
If you zoom out, these environments all share the same traits. ZCM tends to thrive where:
people consume information natively
platforms discourage outbound links
value can be delivered immediately
That describes a huge portion of the modern internet.
Which is why the old playbook — publish content on your website and push people toward it — is becoming less reliable every year.
Influence still happens.
It just happens before the click.
