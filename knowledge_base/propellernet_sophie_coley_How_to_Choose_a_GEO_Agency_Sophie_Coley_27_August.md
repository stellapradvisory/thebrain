How to Choose a GEO Agency
Sophie Coley
27 August 2026
Suddenly, everyone is a GEO agency. Scroll through any agency’s services page and you’ll find “AI Search” or “Generative Engine Optimisation” sitting somewhere near the top, often added in the last few months. Some of that is genuine expertise, but a lot of it is SEO relabelled, with no real change in what’s actually being delivered.
The trouble is, there’s no shared definition yet, no industry standard, no accreditation body checking anyone’s work. So how do you actually tell the difference between an agency that understands this properly and one that’s just moved fast on positioning?
When we’re talking to clients, these are the sorts of questions we’d talk through. GEO is moving fast enough that anyone offering total certainty should probably be the first thing that gives you pause.
Ask what they’re actually optimising for
“AI” isn’t one channel. Optimising for “AI” without specifying which platform is a bit like optimising for “social media” without distinguishing between LinkedIn and TikTok. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Google’s AI Overviews are distinct environments, with different retrieval mechanisms, different training data cutoffs, and different rules about what they will and won’t crawl.
A credible agency should be able to name the specific platforms they’re working across and explain, at least at a high level, how the approach differs between them. If the answer stays at the level of “we optimise for AI,” that’s worth pressing on.
Ask how they measure success, and be wary of a single tidy score
This is probably the single most important question on this list, because it’s the easiest one to get away with answering badly.
Research from Rand Fishkin and Patrick O’Donnell found less than a 1-in-100 chance that two runs of the same AI prompt return the same brands, and less than a 1-in-1,000 chance they return them in the same order. With that in mind, a single sampled AI response tells you almost nothing on its own – and that’s not a minor caveat, it’s the central challenge that anyone trying to measure AI visibility right now should be focused on.
Any agency reporting a single “AI visibility score” without acknowledging that volatility is either not aware of the problem or not being upfront about it. What you want to hear instead is an agency that tracks multiple signals over time, treats individual responses as directional samples rather than absolutes, and can explain the limitations of their own reporting as readily as its strengths. We do this ourselves through Ebb, our AI visibility tool, and we’d expect any agency worth hiring to have an equivalent answer.
Ask for evidence of real citation wins, not just theory
“We understand GEO / AI search / AEO” (or whatever you want to call it!) is easy to say. Proof looks different with named clients, specific platforms, specific outcomes. A citation rate that moved. An AI Overview slot that was won and can be shown. A share-of-voice gain against a named competitor.
When we rebuilt PureGym’s content strategy around AI Overviews, the result was concrete and their blog now appears in over 7,000 Google AI Overviews, roughly 10 times more than their nearest competitor. When we worked on Remitly’s Immigration Index, the campaign page went on to hold the number one AI Overview slot worldwide for high-intent terms like “immigration index.” That’s the kind of specific, checkable evidence worth asking for, from us or from anyone else. If an agency can only offer general claims about capability rather than named, verifiable results, that’s a gap worth noting.
Ask for a definition of fan-out queries, and the difference between ranking and being selected
This is a good litmus test question, because it filters out agencies that have relabelled their SEO offering without genuinely engaging with how AI platforms work.
When an LLM answers a prompt, it doesn’t just search the original phrase. It breaks it into several sub-queries, fan-out queries and searches those instead. A good GEO agency should be able to explain this mechanism to you, unprompted and describe how they identify and target these queries rather than just the obvious keyword.
It’s also worth asking about the difference between ranking and being selected. A page can rank well for a fan-out query and still never get cited, because the title and snippet didn’t clearly signal relevance to what the AI was trying to answer. If an agency’s explanation stops at “we help you rank,” rather than addressing what happens after that, they may not have gone deep enough into how citation actually works.
Ask how GEO fits with their existing SEO and content work
We are not starting from scratch and neither should any credible GEO strategy.
