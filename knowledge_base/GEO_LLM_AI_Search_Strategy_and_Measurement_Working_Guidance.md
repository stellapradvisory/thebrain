# GEO, LLM Visibility, AI Search and Measurement — Working Guidance

*Trusted-source synthesis: Sophie Coley / Propellernet*

*Source collection: Six Sophie Coley articles published by Propellernet between 20 May and 18 August 2026; each original URL is retained in the references below.*

*Added to Stella PR Advisory knowledge base: 20 August 2026*

*Use: Evidence-aware planning and evaluation for GEO, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, LLM visibility, AI-search content, digital PR, and measurement.*

## Purpose and evidential status

This synthesis consolidates the applicable strategy and measurement learning from all six Sophie Coley articles in Propellernet’s AI category available on 20 August 2026. It is intended to support research, briefing, planning, diagnostics, and reporting across **SEO, AI search, GEO, LLM visibility, content, and earned-first strategy**.

> Treat the reported case results, platform snapshots, citation data, and product announcements as **context-bound practitioner evidence**. Use the strategic principles as testable hypotheses, not as universal ranking factors or guarantees of an AI Overview, AI Mode, LLM citation, traffic outcome, or commercial result.

Before applying any recommendation, define the client’s audience, commercial or reputation goal, market, priority query set, current authority, and target platform. Preserve the platform/model, prompt wording, date, location/language, device, personalisation assumptions, and collection method in all analysis.

## Core strategic guidance

| Working principle | Application to strategy | Evidence and guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| **Build from credible existing authority.** | Start by identifying high-value topics where the brand already has relevant, trusted, ranking content. Strengthen accuracy, freshness, completeness, internal linking, direct answers, descriptive headings, comparisons, and decision support before commissioning redundant pages. | A PureGym case observation links refreshed, structured content with AI Overview inclusion. It does not isolate causality or publish a comparable baseline. [1] |
| **Make content genuinely useful and extractable.** | Where appropriate, provide a precise but qualified direct answer, then explain definitions, recommendation criteria, trade-offs, evidence, and limitations. Use tables, FAQs, and structured sections only when they improve human comprehension. | Structure and explicit reasoning are practitioner hypotheses, not fixed citation or ranking factors. [1] [5] [6] |
| **Plan from audiences and scenarios, not assumed prompt volume.** | Use search listening, customer research, reviews, communities, and first-hand audience language to map needs, lifestyles, situations, triggers, labels, constraints, and context. Build content opportunities from those scenarios. | Prompt-volume sources can be synthetic, incomplete, or unable to distinguish search from generative intent. Treat them as qualified directional inputs only. [5] |
| **Treat each AI engine as a separate environment.** | Choose priority engines according to the audience and use case. Audit brand presence, cited domains, source types, and search/fan-out behaviour separately for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Google AI surfaces, and other relevant tools. | A single-time dataset found limited cross-engine citation overlap. This supports engine-specific investigation, not a universal rule about source selection. [3] [4] |
| **Create evidence assets worth citing.** | Invest in original research, transparent indexes, tools, calculators, explainers, comparisons, and localised resources only when they solve a real audience decision or information gap. Publish methodology, source detail, updates, and limitations clearly. | The Remitly case connects a more developed evidence asset with coverage and visibility, but cannot demonstrate direct causation. [1] |
| **Earn authentic independent corroboration.** | Use a clear canonical name for a durable asset or methodology across owned channels. Pursue relevant independent coverage because the material is useful; assess mention accuracy, relevance, diversity, prominence, context, and source quality. | Consistent naming may assist association, but is not a confirmed AI ranking factor. [1] |
| **Anticipate conversational, multimodal, and agent-mediated journeys.** | Address detailed, contextual questions; keep product and service information clear; maintain accurate product feeds; and make key journeys accessible and usable. Validate feature availability by market before major implementation. | Traffic, discoverability, and agent-access implications are hypotheses to test against current rollout conditions. [6] |

## Recommended workflow

### 1. Diagnose before creating

Map priority audience questions, commercial or reputation objectives, current organic visibility, relevant AI-surface incidence, existing cited sources, competitor presence, and available first-party evidence. Segment opportunities into **existing authority to strengthen**, **credible content gap**, **evidence asset opportunity**, and **earned-authority opportunity**.

### 2. Model audience decision scenarios

Move beyond a keyword-only list. For each scenario, document the audience’s need, context, trigger, decision criteria, terminology, likely constraints, and likely follow-up questions. Use representative prompt variations to probe how engines explain and source answers, while recognising that no prompt set is a complete record of demand. [5]

### 3. Improve or create decision-supporting assets

Prefer substantive improvement to a suitable existing asset over shallow net-new “AI content.” Each priority asset should be explicit about **who it serves, what it covers, why it is useful, and why a recommendation follows**. When relevant, add transparent comparisons, definitions, data sources, methodology, examples, update information, author/reviewer expertise, and limitations. [1] [5] [6]

### 4. Design a first-party evidence and digital-PR plan

For data- or research-led work, agree the methodology, quality controls, documentation, stakeholder value, localisation, accessibility, maintenance plan, canonical asset name, and earned-media targets upfront. Measure independent corroboration by quality and accuracy, rather than raw coverage volume alone. [1] [3]

### 5. Target source ecosystems by engine and category

Do not assume a visibility tactic transfers across engines. Audit which domain and source types are cited for the category on each priority platform, then judge whether the right response is stronger owned content, a maintained public knowledge footprint, relevant independent coverage, specialist third-party sources, community participation, video, or a combination. Re-test observed patterns over time. [3] [4]

### 6. Test, document, and update

Establish a query/prompt baseline, run repeated observations, keep the methodology and raw outputs where permitted, and compare patterns over time. Attribute cautiously because content changes, PR, site authority, product availability, seasonality, market conditions, and platform changes may all affect results.

## Measurement framework

### Avoid one universal AI-visibility score

A blended score can conceal important differences in retrieval behaviour, citations, response ordering, and user intent. Use a layered framework that separates **response-level observation**, **citation and source analysis**, **fan-out/search signals**, **organic performance**, **earned authority**, and **business or reputation outcomes**. [2] [3] [4]

| Measurement layer | Record | Interpretation guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| **Scenario and query frame** | Audience scenario, intent, prompt/query, prompt version, platform/model, market, date/time, location, language, device, and personalisation assumption. | Do not compare results gathered under different conditions without making the difference explicit. |
| **Response-level visibility** | Brand mention/presence, answer context, list inclusion and observed order, citations/links, competitor presence, and raw response capture where appropriate. | Repeated answers may vary significantly. Report samples and trends, not a definitive permanent rank. [2] |
| **Citation and source ecosystem** | Cited domain, source type, page/asset, frequency, citation context, source overlap by engine, and representation accuracy. | Separate citations from brand mentions. Never generalise category- or engine-specific source patterns without testing. [3] |
| **Fan-out and organic signals** | Observable fan-out queries, Google/Bing rankings for them, search volume where relevant, ranking trends, impressions, clicks, links, and owned-asset quality. | Fan-out visibility can be a useful upstream diagnostic; it is not proof of later AI citation or inclusion. [2] [4] |
| **Earned authority** | Earned mention, canonical-name accuracy, source relevance, editorial prominence, geography/language, context, sentiment, referral traffic, and relevant links. | Coverage volume alone does not demonstrate AI influence. [1] |
| **Business and reputation** | Qualified traffic, leads, conversion proxy, assisted conversion, branded demand, reputation signals, and decision-maker outcomes. | Report contribution and uncertainty; avoid single-surface attribution claims. |

### Repeated sampling and response volatility

The source set highlights substantial variation in repeated AI-answer brand lists and ordering. One response, citation count, or observed list position is therefore not conclusive evidence. Use a version-controlled, representative prompt set across multiple runs; preserve the number, timing, and rules of each run; and report distributions, volatility, directionality, and exceptions instead of a headline average alone. [2]

### Fan-out diagnostics and Reciprocal Rank Fusion

Where the platform or measurement method reveals web searches/fan-out queries, collect them alongside response observations. Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF) can be used to aggregate the brand’s positions across multiple fan-out query rankings as an upstream search-visibility diagnostic. It should complement—not replace—brand presence, citations, source quality, organic performance, and audience outcomes.

The source article does not set a universal RRF formula, constant, query-generation protocol, sampling cadence, or demonstrate that a higher RRF causes citations. Document all implementation choices and test whether the chosen measure is prospectively associated with the outcomes relevant to the client. [2]

### Minimum auditable data fields

Maintain a structured record containing: `client`, `market`, `audience scenario`, `intent`, `prompt/query`, `prompt version`, `platform/model`, `timestamp`, `location`, `language`, `device`, `personalisation setting`, `brand presence`, `answer/list status`, `citations`, `cited source type`, `fan-out queries`, `fan-out rankings`, `competitors`, `owned asset`, `content or PR intervention`, `organic metrics`, `business metric`, and `analyst note`.

## Hypotheses to test—not fixed platform factors

| Proposition | Suitable validation approach |
|---|---|
| Existing organic authority is associated with AI Overview presence. | Compare matched topic/page cohorts with baseline organic and AI-surface measures while controlling as far as possible for topic, freshness, authority, and seasonality. [1] |
| Clear answer structure and explicit reasoning improve inclusion or citation. | Test substantive content improvements on comparable assets, retain version history, and use repeated measurements. [1] [5] |
| Consistent asset naming across independent coverage improves entity association. | Track accurate independent mentions and compare with source/citation patterns; avoid manufactured coverage and causal claims. [1] |
| Engine-specific source ecosystems need different tactics. | Repeat engine-by-category citation audits and compare source types, content gaps, and interventions over time. [3] [4] |
| Audience-facet coverage better serves AI journeys than prompt-volume targeting. | Compare scenario coverage, answer usefulness, visibility/citation trends, and audience outcomes; do not use unverified prompt volume as a stand-alone KPI. [5] |
| Fan-out ranking/RRF is an early indicator of AI visibility. | Pre-register the RRF method and correlate it prospectively with repeated presence/citation outcomes; report uncertainty and exceptions. [2] |
| Agentic search and AI commerce change site/product-feed requirements. | Segment analysis by platform rollout and market; monitor journeys and conversion outcomes only once changes are genuinely available. [6] |

## Relevance to Stella PR Advisory’s work

Apply this material in the **Brand Visibility Across Search & AI** layer of Stella’s work and within broader earned-media measurement. It supports a disciplined approach in which useful owned information, independent corroboration, search performance, AI-surface observations, and business or reputation outcomes are evaluated together.

Do not promise AI Overview placements, citations, visibility scores, traffic gains, or revenue outcomes. Do not treat a content format, a source type, PR coverage volume, a single AI response, an opaque visibility score, or a platform snapshot as sufficient causal evidence.
