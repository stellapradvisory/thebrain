# giffgaff Kantar Brand Health (Q1 2026) — Search & Reputation Opportunities
*Prepared for Stella Bayles*

This Kantar debrief is a gift for your positioning with giffgaff. It explicitly validates that search behaviour is a core measure of brand health, and it diagnoses exactly why giffgaff is struggling to convert visibility into consideration. 

Here are the three biggest opportunities for Splendid to pitch search and online reputation work based on this data:

## 1. The "Visibility vs. Meaning" Gap
**The Kantar finding:** giffgaff has strong awareness and performs well on being "Different" (104 index), but it lags significantly on being "Meaningful" (92 index) compared to Tesco Mobile (106) and Lebara. The report states plainly: *"this helps explain why visibility is not converting into growth: the brand is being noticed but not always chosen."*

**The Reputation Opportunity:** 
If giffgaff is noticed but not chosen, the problem is happening in the evaluation phase — which is entirely driven by search and AI. When people search to validate their choice, they aren't finding enough reassurance.
* **The Pitch:** An earned-first reputation strategy designed specifically to bridge the gap between Awareness and Consideration. By dominating AI answers and search results with third-party proof, community advocacy, and independent validation, Splendid can make giffgaff's "Difference" feel safe enough to choose.

## 2. The Reassurance Deficit
**The Kantar finding:** Tesco Mobile is winning by leaning into "reassurance-led messaging" (peace of mind, predictability), while giffgaff is relying on "playful comms" and deals. Crucially, giffgaff is seeing year-on-year declines in search share for high-trust categories like *Accessibility & Support* (-13), *Handsets & Contracts* (-13), and *Roaming* (-26). Meanwhile, Lebara is attacking them directly on flexibility and value.

**The Reputation Opportunity:**
Playful comms win attention, but reassurance wins the search box. When a customer searches *"is giffgaff reliable?"* or *"giffgaff vs Tesco mobile coverage"*, playful ads don't help. 
* **The Pitch:** A targeted search reputation programme to own the "reassurance" narrative online. This means auditing what AI engines and Google say about giffgaff's support, roaming, and contract fairness, and using PR to seed objective, trust-building content into the publishers that AI engines cite most.

## 3. "Fairness" Requires Proof, Not Just Slogans
**The Kantar finding:** The report identifies "Fairness" as giffgaff's most powerful differentiating platform, but comes with a massive warning: it must be executed *credibly and humbly*. Kantar explicitly says credibility comes from "tangible actions, not brand slogans" and that a "collective, customer-inclusive approach feels more credible."

**The Reputation Opportunity:**
Fairness cannot be self-declared; it has to be earned and validated by others. If giffgaff just runs ads saying "we are fair," the switched-on 18-35 audience will immediately search to verify it.
* **The Pitch:** Splendid can build the "Fairness Proof Engine." Instead of relying on owned channels, use earned media, Reddit community management, and targeted digital PR to ensure that when AI engines are asked *"which mobile network is the fairest?"*, they cite independent reviews, forum consensus, and trusted tech journalists pointing to giffgaff.
