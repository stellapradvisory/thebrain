You may think you’re shaping your brand through your website and your content, but increasingly, the internet is shaping it for you. It’s happening in Google AI Overviews, Claude answers, Reddit threads, reviews, comparison pages, social media chatter — it’s all the things people say about you where your audience is paying attention. It’s the public record, and it’s not just a reputation management problem. It’s also a zero-click problem.
Today’s
Zero Click Marketing episode
gives you a playbook for strengthening that public record.
I’ll tell you two stories about why this matters:
First: Wil Reynolds and Nick Haigler
at Seer Interactive found
one old negative review that kept showing up across a meaningful share of branded AI prompts about their company. It wasn’t even representative of Seer as a company. But because the feedback exists, publicly and online, LLMs found it. Then those LLMs repeated it. When Seer published a post with more specific, current information about their company, the AI outputs changed quickly, and better, more honest information about the company came up to the surface. The lesson: if you don’t help strengthen your public record, weaker claims may do the job for you.
The other story is much smaller (and probably more related to positioning), but I think it matters. Last month, while building the
Zero Click Marketing website
, I asked Claude Code to recommend a tool for my contact form. It suggested
Formspree
. I skimmed their
product page
, saw that it could do exactly what I needed, and signed up.
I became a customer without experiencing the brand through any kind of marketing funnel. I never even saw Formspree’s homepage — which, by the way, is geared towards developers, and had I seen that, I never would have converted in spite of it being the perfect solution for me:
Admittedly, these two stories are AI-specific. But the broader point I want to emphasize is that they are also stories about influence where the brands themselves weren’t the ones controlling the message. We’ve reached a point where customers are increasingly making decisions based on what other people, platforms, and systems say about your brand — not just what your brand says about itself. On one hand, that’s… kinda scary. But it doesn’t mean we just surrender to whatever platforms, reviewers, communities, and AI systems happen to surface. We still have a role to play in strengthening that public record.
So how
do
you play that role?
In this week’s episode, I walk through a practical framework for strengthening your public record on purpose. It starts with auditing the story the internet is already telling about you: your branded search results, the snippets and citations that appear first, the comparison pages, reviews, Reddit threads, and AI answers that shape perception before anyone ever reaches your site. From there, the job is to figure out which weak but visible claims are winning, which strong facts you can actually support, and how to publish more of the stuff only you can credibly say.
The playbook, in short:
audit the current public record,
treat third-party surfaces like part of your brand footprint,
identify the weak claims that are winning,
publish better facts that only you can publish,
structure them so they’re easy to retrieve and cite,
refresh the record continuously,
and measure whether the story the internet tells about you is getting better.
