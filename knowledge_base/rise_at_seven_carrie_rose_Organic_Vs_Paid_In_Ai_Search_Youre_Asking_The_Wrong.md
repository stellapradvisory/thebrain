Organic vs Paid in AI Search? You're Asking the Wrong Question
AEO Search
Share
For the past 18 months, marketers have obsessed over one question how do we get recommended by ChatGPT?
Today, that question changes.
ChatGPT is no longer just an AI assistant. It’s becoming an advertising platform.
Which means every brand now has two visibility challenges:
How do we earn recommendations? And when do we pay for attention?
The obvious next question follows: is AI search about to become another fight between organic and paid?
We don't think that's the right question.
The bigger one is this: how do we become the brand AI recommends, no matter where that recommendation comes from?
AI search is following a familiar path
We've seen this story before.
When Google introduced paid search, plenty of people predicted SEO would die out...
It didn't. Search got more sophisticated instead.
Organic and paid didn't replace each other. They grew up together. The brands that won weren't the ones with the biggest SEO budgets or the deepest paid media pockets. They were the ones who understood how each channel fed the other, and built a strategy around total search visibility.
AI search looks like it's heading the same way. Right now, tools like ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity build their recommendations from signals like:
Trusted publishers
Earned media
Reviews
Reddit discussions
Forums
Creator content
Expert commentary
Authoritative websites
If paid placements join that mix, they won't replace these signals. They'll just become one more input into how brands get discovered.
The opportunity isn't to optimise one channel. It's to optimise the whole ecosystem.
Matt Holmes, Executive Director, Integrated Strategy
How do LLMs currently discover and weight information? What changes if paid placements become another input, rather than a replacement for existing signals? The way LLMs understand the relationship between entities is largely driven by offsite signals - their view of the world is about mathematical closeness to the prompt rather than the same type of understanding as more mature search engine knowledge graphs.
LLMS don’t have their own index of sites as Google does. A study by Muck Rack of more than 25 million links showed that 84% of AI citations are from earned media, with just 0.3% being driven by paid. What this doesn’t consider is that sometimes what looks like earned media is actually an affiliate link, and AI doesn’t currently distinguish between the two, which could end up skewing results, particularly in the world of ecom. LLMs may remedy this in future models because, like early Google, they’ll want to be known as a trusted and unbiased source which serves up the best results and keeps people coming back. If you look at the backlash ChatGPT caught when they first trialled paid ads on the platform, having paid influence the results (or even just be alongside organic results) will need to be managed very carefully - modern users are far more cynical than when Google launched AdWords nearly 26 years ago.
AI doesn't care which team made the signal
The biggest challenge here isn't technical. It's organisational.
Most marketing teams are still built around channels. SEO owns rankings. Digital PR owns coverage. Paid owns media. Social owns engagement. Content owns the website.
AI doesn't see marketing that way.
