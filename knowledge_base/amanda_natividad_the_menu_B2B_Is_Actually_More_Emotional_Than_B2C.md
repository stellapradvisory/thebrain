We’re used to thinking that B2B transactions are logical, and consumer-facing ones are emotional. That consumers buy on feeling — the perfume, the convertible, the thing that promises to make them someone. Businesses buy based on spreadsheets, procurement reviews, a feature matrix, and a cool head.
But my good friend Talia Wolf says it’s the other way around. When she said that to me in today’s
Zero Click Marketing episode
, my eyes lit up as I eagerly waited her rationale.
Talia is a conversion optimization expert, the founder of GetUplift, and the author of
Emotional Targeting
. She’s spent years digging into why people actually buy — not the reason they give, the real one. Her claim is blunt: a B2B purchase carries more emotional weight than almost anything you’ll buy for yourself, because the stakes are personal in a way a new cardigan never is.
Think about what’s actually riding on it. Buy something dumb for yourself and you’re out a little money and some pride. Pick the wrong software for your company and the fallout has your name on it — the budget for the year, and your standing with everyone who signed off. Talia’s example is the one that stuck with me. You’ve been told to switch email platforms. Now you’re up at 2am eyes wide, catastrophizing the what-ifs:
What if nobody can use the new one? What if everybody hates it? What if you migrate and the whole list vanishes in some freak accident, and everyone knows it was your fault?
That’s not a rational buyer.
Talia splits it into two ideas:
Self-image: how I want to feel about myself.
Social image: how I want everyone else to see me.
The person choosing enterprise software doesn’t just want the product to work. They want to feel like they made the right call — and they want everyone else to see that too. None of that lives in a feature list. Brands lead with “we have AI” and “all-in-one platform,” then wonder why their genuinely good product isn’t clicking with anyone.
If you’re competing on features and pricing, you’re probably already in a knife fight with five companies that look a lot like you. Once a buyer confirms you fit the budget and check the boxes, the thing that tips them toward you over the other two finalists isn’t another spec. It’s whether you make them feel understood: whether you clearly know what they’ve tried before, why it failed them, and what’s keeping them up at night.
In our interview, Talia gets specific about how you actually pull that off — including a comparison page she rebuilt around what an agency owner is really afraid of (not “here’s our profitability report,” but “here’s how you’ll finally know whether you can take on the next client”) that drove 54% more free trials from organic. We get into why “save time” is an empty promise until you know whose time, and what they’d do with it once they got it back. And why hiding your pricing in 2026 just hands your story to ChatGPT, which will cheerfully get it wrong on your behalf.
It’s one of my favorite conversations we’ve had about the part of marketing that lives underneath the funnel — the actual human on the other end of the purchase, doing math about their own job.
