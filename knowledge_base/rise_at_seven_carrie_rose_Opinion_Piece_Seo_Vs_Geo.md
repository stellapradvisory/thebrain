Opinion Piece: The SEO vs GEO debate is making our industry look stupid
Share
We are some of the smartest people in the marketing industry, and right now we look stupid.
AI search/discovery has kicked off an identity crisis in marketing. A debate!
And whilst many think there's this "new way of doing marketing" and everyone needs to learn it
FAST
...
I'm going to share a really strong opinion that it's actually always been this way, nothing's changed - it's just exposing which ones are clear, consistent and credible - to then be worth recommending.
Here's why...
There are people who work in brand marketing
STEALING
our lunch money right now.
YES REALLY.
They're taking search budgets, they're acting like they know how to create the most discoverable brands. This is our space, our game, we own this. But why is it SEO is losing?
Whilst everyone is debating "what to call it - SEO or GEO - and is GEO just good SEO" - the problem isn't what we call it, it's that we're (still) asking the wrong question.
I'm not seeing enough SEOs ask - ok, what do we
ACTUALLY
need to do to be clear, consistent and credible? Worthy enough to be recommended by AI?
I am seeing studies left, right and centre telling us things we
SHOULD
already know ...
That AI prefers others' recommendations (which is why Reddit is the most cited platform) (
well yeah, surely we can guess that?)
That media/earned media/PR drives citations/influences them (
we knew this, we just forget that media holds HUGE trust)
That AI loves fresh content less than 13 weeks old
(My mum could have told you that)
That reviews and real opinion impact citations
(Common sense - or is that not common?)
I mean it, I'm sounding harsh, but sometimes I think, come on guys - ask ourselves. What do we want to see? Where would we trust recommendations?
Did we really need to spend THOUSANDS of £/$ to tell us things we should know?
We as consumers are DONE with brand heavy, keyword stuffed, manipulated content we just want someone real to tell us
"whats the best skin care for dry skin?" Or "the best airfryer to get that isn't going to cost more than my mortgage?"
And the fact is - not one CMO has ever asked me whether we do "SEO or GEO." They ask me one question:
why is my competitor showing up in ChatGPT and I'm not?
And while the industry argues about acronyms on LinkedIn, that question is going unanswered.
We've seen this film before
Every time the ground shifts, our industry does the same thing. Invents a new three-letter acronym. Launches a wave of tools. Sells the same audit with a new cover slide. I get why. New acronym = new budget line = new pitch. I run an agency. I understand the commercial temptation better than most.
We did it with voice search. We did it with mobile. Half the "GEO agencies" that appeared in the last 18 months are SEO agencies that changed their homepage headline. I said it in this
video here
- they're just chasing keywords because that's what SEO has always done
But here's my problem with it: GEO, as it's being sold, is mostly SEO tactics with worse measurement. Schema. Crawlability. Content structure.
"Optimise your headings so the LLM can extract them." That's not a new discipline. That's technical SEO wearing a lanyard that says AI on it. It is - and we know it!
And the bits of "GEO" that ARE genuinely new? They're not optimisation at all.
