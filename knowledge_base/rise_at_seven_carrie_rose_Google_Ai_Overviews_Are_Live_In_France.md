AI Overviews Just Landed in France. Is Your Brand Part of the Answer?
Share
It's finally here for France.
Google has switched on AI Overviews in France. Search a category question now, and an AI-generated summary sits above the traditional results, often followed by AI Mode
For French brands, this isn't another algorithm update to shrug off. It changes the question that matters. One the rest of the world has already been facing. It used to be: does our brand rank on Google?
Now it's: when Google explains our category, does it mention us, recommend us, and get us right?
That's not an SEO problem anymore. It's a brand, content and earned media problem.
The new front page of search
Ask Google "quelle est la meilleure banque pour épargner" or "quelle marque de valise est la plus fiable" and you won't get ten blue links first.
You'll get a synthesised answer based on a combination of sources that AI is citing from.
Google says the fundamentals still matter: crawlable pages, clean structured data, genuinely helpful content.
And we agree, but no brand builds authority by simply insisting it has some. Google calls the process behind AI Overviews "query fan-out": dozens of related searches run behind the scenes, pulling from far more than your own site. Which means what the rest of the internet says about you now carries real weight.
Your reputation is being written off-site
Press coverage, expert commentary, product reviews, comparison articles, forums, podcasts: all of it feeds the picture Google assembles. Your website tells AI what you claim to be. Everything else tells them whether that claim holds up.
Backlinks still count, but treating Digital PR as a pure link-building exercise misses the point now.
Good earned media does three things at once. It signals authority through real expertise, demonstrated with original research or useful commentary rather than asserted in an About page.
It builds relevance, tying your brand to the specific topics you want to be known for rather than scattering generic mentions. And it keeps the story consistent, so your site, the press and review platforms aren't quietly contradicting each other.
None of this is about gaming an answer. It's about making sure the accurate version of your brand is the one available wherever people are researching.
France plays by different rules when it comes to PR
A campaign translated into French isn't a French campaign. France runs on data and cultural moments specifically relating to them and their culture, not a press release with the currency swapped.
Our
French Digital PR
team has found the market rewards specific things consistently: proprietary research, timely commentary tied to real news, strong regional angles, and stories grounded in how French consumers actually behave.
That's earned coverage in Elle, Grazia, Gala, HuffPost, Biba, Le Parisien, Le Figaro, Le Monde, Ouest-France and MCE for clients including SIXT, JD Sports, SharkNinja, Preply and Raisin. Volume alone won't buy that kind of authority. A hundred throwaway mentions won't outweigh a handful of pieces in the outlets that actually shape your category and brand perception.
