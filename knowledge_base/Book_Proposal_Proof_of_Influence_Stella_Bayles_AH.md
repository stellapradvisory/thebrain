1. Biography 

Stella Bayles is a communications strategist, PR measurement specialist, speaker, author, 
and industry educator best known for helping modernise how the public relations 
industry measures impact and demonstrates value. She is currently Head of Industry 
Relations at CoverageBook, where she works at the intersection of modern PR practice, 
stakeholder reporting, and AI-ready measurement. 

With more than 20 years of experience spanning agency, consultancy, in-house, and PR 
technology roles, Stella has built a reputation as one of the industry’s leading advocates 
for smarter, more accessible PR measurement. Before joining CoverageBook, she spent 
over 14 years agency-side, initially in traditional public relations before becoming one of 
the early practitioners in Europe to combine SEO strategy with PR campaigns and 
communications measurement. Her work helped major brands better connect 
communications activity with business outcomes, contributing to signiﬁcant client growth, 
multiple industry award wins, and substantial agency revenue growth.

At CoverageBook, Stella works closely with PR teams globally and has ﬁrsthand visibility 
into how communications professionals are adapting reporting, measurement, and AI 
workﬂows in real time. Her work focuses on helping PR teams move away from vanity 
metrics and retrospective reporting towards structured, insight-led measurement systems 
that support stronger strategic decision-making and clearer evidence of organisational 
impact. 

She regularly speaks internationally on topics including PR analytics, communications 
measurement, data literacy, AI in PR, reporting strategy, and the integration of PR with 
wider marketing disciplines. Stella has spoken at major industry events including PRSA 
ICON, AMEC conferences, PRCA events, PRWeek forums, and international 
communications conferences across the UK, Europe, and North America. 

Stella is also the host of the long-running PR Resolution Podcast, where she interviews 
leaders across PR, communications, SEO, analytics, and marketing technology. Through 
podcasting, webinars, conference speaking, industry training, and university guest 
lecturing, she has educated thousands of communications professionals internationally. 

She is the author of PR’s Digital Resolution (2016), an early guide exploring digital PR, 
SEO integration, and communications measurement. She has also contributed to industry 
initiatives and educational programmes with organisations including AMEC and the 
PRCA, particularly around data literacy, communications evaluation, and the future of PR 
measurement. 

Known for translating complex measurement concepts into practical, accessible 
guidance, Stella advocates for a more evidence-led PR industry where communications 
professionals feel conﬁdent using structured insight to improve strategy, demonstrate 
value, and adapt to the future of AI-assisted communications.

4. Detailed Book Vision 

PR measurement was never supposed to become this intimidating. 

Somewhere along the way, the communications industry turned measurement into something 
that felt overly technical, specialist, and disconnected from the reality of modern PR work. At 
exactly the same time, PR teams started being asked tougher strategic questions than ever 
before. 

Today, communications professionals are expected to prove value, demonstrate organisational 
impact, contribute to commercial decision making, and explain what is inﬂuencing outcomes in 
real time. But much of the industry is still relying on measurement systems built for a far simpler  
and traditional media landscape. 

PR is no longer just newspaper coverage, broadcast interviews, and end-of-campaign reports. 

Inﬂuence now happens across creators, communities, podcasts, newsletters, Reddit threads, 
LinkedIn conversations, YouTube videos, stakeholder networks, and increasingly, AI-generated 
search experiences. Audiences discover information diﬀerently, trust information diﬀerently, and 
engage with brands diﬀerently than they did even a few years ago. 

Yet despite this shift, much of PR measurement still focuses on counting outputs rather than 
understanding inﬂuence. 

Reporting often remains retrospective. Dashboards become overloaded with disconnected 
metrics. And too many PR professionals still feel like measurement belongs to analysts or 
“numbers people” instead of the people closest to the communications work itself. 

That gap matters because PR has never had more strategic opportunity than it does right now. 

Communications teams increasingly inﬂuence:

The book argues that one of the biggest mistakes the PR industry has historically made is trying

underlying communications data. 

Modern PR measurement should not be about forcing communications into false certainty. 

Instead, it should function as: 
 “A system of evidence for attempts at inﬂuence.” 

That distinction is important because PR does not operate in isolation and inﬂuence is rarely 
linear. Communications activity contributes to wider organisational outcomes alongside 
marketing, sales, product, customer experience, leadership, and market conditions. 

The goal is not to claim PR caused every business result. 
The goal is to build credible, structured systems that help organisations better understand: 

●  what is gaining traction 
●  what is inﬂuencing outcomes 
●  what patterns are emerging 
●  and where strategy should evolve next

6. Competitive analysis  

 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Proof of Influence: How Modern PR Teams Measure What Matters 

There are already several respected PR measurement books in the market, including titles by 
Tom Watson, Paul Noble, David Michaelson, Don Stacks, and Gini Dietrich. However, many of the 
core books still shaping industry thinking today were published before some of the biggest shifts 
in modern communications took place. 

Since then, PR has changed dramatically. 

Audiences increasingly consume information through creators, social platforms, podcasts, 
newsletters, and AI generated search environments rather than traditional publisher led journeys. 
PR activity now spans multiple formats and platforms, yet much of the industry still relies on 
measurement approaches built for a more traditional media environment. 

At the same time, communications professionals are increasingly expected to provide strategic 
insight, evidence of organisational impact, and commercially relevant recommendations, while 
also navigating growing pressure around AI adoption and data readiness. 

This creates a clear gap in the current publishing market. 

While newer PR books tend to focus on broader communications strategy, branding, digital 
marketing, or AI theory, there are very few practical books focused speciﬁcally on helping 
everyday PR professionals build simple, modern measurement systems for the AI era. 

That is where Proof of Inﬂuence is diﬀerent. 

Rather than approaching measurement from an academic or analyst perspective, the book is 
written from my (Stella Bayles’) own experience moving from creative PR practitioner to one of 
the industry’s leading voices on modern PR measurement and communications insight. 

Its core philosophy is that measurement should no longer be a retrospective reporting task 
owned by specialists, but an everyday strategic skill that helps PR professionals understand what 
is working, improve communications activity faster, strengthen stakeholder conﬁdence, and prove 
inﬂuence more credibly in the modern communications environment. 

Unlike traditional PR measurement books written primarily for evaluation specialists, or academic 
audiences, Proof of Inﬂuence is written for the wider PR industry (the real PR professional!). It 
combines practical implementation, accessible language, AI-era relevance, and structured 
strategic thinking for modern communications teams working across fragmented media 
environments.  

The book positions measurement not as a specialist reporting function, but as an everyday 
capability that helps PR professionals make better decisions, communicate value more clearly, 
and adapt faster in a changing communications landscape.
