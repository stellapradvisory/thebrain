This Month in Paid Media: July 2026
Nicolas Rentschler
4 August 2026
AI is increasingly shaping every corner of the ad experience itself, from the moment someone sees an ad to the moment they buy – moving further from the backend of paid media into the front end.
Platforms are moving fast to close the gap between discovery and purchase, and to make the mechanics of relevance and bidding more visible than ever.
Here are four updates that stood out to us in July.
1. Amazon launches Alexa+ Agentic Ads
Amazon has introduced
Alexa+ Agentic Ads
, letting shoppers go straight from seeing an ad to completing the purchase without ever leaving Alexa.
In practice, this means:
The path from impression to purchase can now happen entirely within a single voice interaction
Retailers and advertisers need to think about how their products are discovered and bought when there’s no webpage in between
This is the clearest signal yet that the agentic commerce conversation has moved from theory to product.
2. Google tests AI-generated summaries inside Search ads
Google is testing AI-generated summaries within Search ads, using AI to sum up what an ad is actually offering right inside the results.
The potential impact is clear:
Ad copy may be interpreted and rewritten by AI before a searcher ever reads the original wording
Clarity and structure in ad copy could matter more than clever phrasing
Advertisers may have less direct control over the exact words that appear next to their brand
It’s a small test for now, but one worth watching closely to see how this changes the way we write ad copy going forward.
3. Google tests Strongest Match labels on Search ads
Google is also testing “Strongest Match” labels on Search ads, showing searchers right there how closely an ad matches what they typed.
The implications are straightforward:
Relevance, once hidden inside the auction, becomes something searchers can see and judge for themselves
Weaker-matching ads may need to work harder to earn the click once labelled
Relevance used to be hidden in the auction. Now it might be on display for everyone to judge, which raises the bar for how precisely campaigns are built.
4. Google announces changes to target-based bid strategies
Google has announced changes to target-based bid strategies landing 17 August, meaning budget-limited campaigns on Target CPA or ROAS will start holding closer to the actual target instead of quietly overachieving.
In practice, this means:
Campaigns that have historically beaten their targets may see performance move closer to the number you actually set
Reported efficiency could look different overnight, even if underlying performance hasn’t changed
Budgets and targets set based on old overachievement patterns may need revisiting before the switch flips
It’s worth checking your numbers before 17 August. If your current targets were set assuming a bit of built-in headroom, that headroom may not be there for much longer.
Looking across these updates, a few themes stand out:
Agentic commerce is moving from concept to reality, shortening the distance between ad and purchase
Relevance and matching, once buried in the mechanics of the auction, are becoming visible to the people the ads are aimed at
Platforms are tightening up how literally they deliver on the targets we set them, rewarding precision over comfortable overachievement
For advertisers, the opportunity lies in how we respond. Staying close to performance data, testing new formats early and revisiting assumptions before platforms change the rules under us will help us make the most of these shifts.
