## Extracted Source Content

I’ve been trying to streamline my skincare routine while still getting solid results. The 10-step Korean skincare routine? It’s lovely, but I no longer have the patience to fit that into my schedule… or into my budget. So I started on a tretinoin prescription, Vanicream Daily Facial Moisturizer twice a day, Medik8 C-Tetra Vitamin C serum in the morning, and Skinfix Triple Lipid-Peptide Cream at night. That’s it.
Oh, and my $400
CurrentBody LED face mask
. Not even to humble-brag, but brag-brag, I look really cool wearing it.
I know the text in this thumbnail isn’t really the point of this post, but how else am I gonna get people to read this?! Also, I do answer this question, so keep reading…
So naturally, I decided to build CurrentBody’s Black Friday Cyber Monday marketing strategy. It’s almost September, so we better get a move on!
CurrentBody’s Series 2 LED face mask currently sells for $469.99 in the U.S., putting it firmly in “I’m going to think about this purchase for a minute” territory. It’s not a $14 sheet mask you toss into your Sephora cart because the packaging was compelling. It’s a beauty device you’re likely to research, compare, rationalize, abandon in your cart, reconsider, watch three YouTube reviews about, and perhaps finally buy when Black Friday gives you permission.
Which makes it an excellent audience research problem.
For this
Audience Research Brief
, I ran a SparkToro report on:
Women in the U.S. seeking at-home LED anti-aging treatments. SparkToro estimates that audience at just over 1 million people, with an estimated market value of $44.9-$56.7 billion.
My question was simple:
If CurrentBody wants to sell a bunch of LED masks during Black Friday/Cyber Monday, what should it be doing before Black Friday arrives?
Because my suspicion is that for a considered purchase like this one, the most important part of your Black Friday marketing happens before the sale. And the audience research supports that.
First: do these LED masks actually do anything?
Important detour!
I am a skincare enthusiast, not a dermatologist. I do not believe my glowing red Hannibal Lecter mask is the fountain of youth. But red-light therapy is also not pure TikTok woo.
The
American Academy of Dermatology says
research suggests red-light LED devices can produce “subtle to noticeable” improvements in fine lines and wrinkles, discoloration, roughness, and loose skin, while also cautioning that at-home devices vary and that more research is needed to determine optimal treatment protocols.
There are randomized trials behind the category, too. In
one 2023 randomized trial
of 137 women ages 40-65, ten sessions of red or amber photobiomodulation over four weeks produced about a 30% reduction in measured wrinkle volume around the eyes. The treatments did not significantly improve every measure studied, including hydration and elasticity — a useful reminder that “supported by evidence” and “magic face time machine” are very different claims.
So, there is legitimate science here. But there are also plenty of reasons for a $470 shopper to have questions.
Which brings us back to the marketing.
Insight #1: This audience uses social media. They just don’t over-index on it.
Here’s something I would have guessed incorrectly. If you asked me where women interested in anti-aging skincare devices spend their time, I’d reflexively say Instagram. TikTok. Pinterest. YouTube.
And yes! They use all of those places.
According to our report, 87.9% of this audience uses YouTube monthly. About 76% use Facebook, 75% Instagram, 75% Reddit, and 56% TikTok.
But compared with the average U.S. web user, this audience under-indexes on every one of those platforms. YouTube is about 8% below average. Facebook 12%. Instagram 5%. Reddit 7%. TikTok 13%. LinkedIn, X, and Pinterest are even further below their respective U.S. averages.
That does not mean CurrentBody should pump the brakes on social media. After all, 75% audience reach on Instagram is… 75% audience reach!
This is where marketers can get tripped up by affinity data. Reach tells you whether a channel matters. Indexing tells you whether this audience is unusually concentrated there.
Instagram could absolutely belong in the media plan. But the data doesn’t support the lazy version of the strategy: Beauty product → beauty influencer → Instagram → done.
Next up: this audience’s information diet gets much more interesting when we look beyond social.
Insight #2: Apparently the LED-mask girlies are on Claude?!
About 37% of this audience uses ChatGPT monthly, only slightly above the U.S. average.
But Claude? 16.9% of this audience uses Claude, versus about 5.3% of the U.S. web population.
That’s a 221% over-index.
Gemini also slightly over-indexes, while most of the broader AI category does not. In fact, AI and machine-learning websites as a category account for a smaller share of this audience’s browsing than they do for the average web user.
